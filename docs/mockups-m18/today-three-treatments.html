Round 5c — both themes, reviewable on device. Every treatment always shipped light and dark tokens, but the page had no way to flip them: on your phone you only ever saw whichever one iOS was in. The Appearance control up top now forces Light or Dark (System follows the phone), and forcing a theme is verified to render byte-identical to the OS in that mode — so what you review is what an owner of that device sees. Three things came out of auditing both sides. (1) Contrast: every text-contrast failure in the whole file was in light mode — 34 of them, the muted caption tone and the element hues at small sizes, worst 3.07:1 on Daybreak's "Watch" heading. The light hues are now 5–15% deeper (same hue, same character) and all six treatments clear WCAG AA in both themes on every screen. (2) The black anchor doesn't survive dark mode. Trail, Forecast and Compass II each stake their identity on one near-black mass per screen (agency line, today pill, nav) — in dark that mass sank into the ground and read as a hole. The anchor now inverts: in dark it becomes a paper mass with ink text, so it stays the boldest object on the screen rather than the darkest. Daybreak and Sky needed nothing (no black anchor; Sky's anchor is the green agency card). (3) Two rules were themed only for the OS media query, so the toggle couldn't reach them — Compass's Worth-doing/Worth-postponing columns and Sky's nav shadow. Fixed.

M18 mockups, round 5 — Trail is the freestyle concept: the day as a route on a topographic map, because the reading copy already speaks in terrain (“pothole sign on the road,” “a stretch worth taking slower,” “the day runs crosswise”). New palette (olive topo-paper, pine ink, map-legend element hues), new type (Bricolage Grotesque display + Space Mono map labels, Figtree body), new assets: a contour-map hero where the dragon–dog clash is a marked crossing and the favorable window is a green-blazed stretch; a 7-day elevation profile; blaze/cairn signage for Favors/Watch; the agency line as a directional trail sign. The orbit logo doubles as the compass rose; black stays the one anchor per screen (you-are-here pill, signpost, nav). Waypoints hang off a dashed rail — the page itself is the trail. Round 5b (owner: make the palette dynamic per day): the terrain palette is now keyed to the day pillar's element — five terrains (Wood olive forest, Fire blush canyon, Earth loess dune, Metal porcelain granite, Water nautical chart), deterministic per date like everything else; the element data hues, black anchors, and green/amber signage stay constant so meaning never shifts, only the paper and ink do. Zodiac-keyed palettes were considered and set aside: 12 grounds blur into sameness and the animal already owns the day's identity as the map silhouette. The dashed “DAY PALETTE” capsule under the date is mockup-only preview chrome (the fixture day is locked to Wood) — the real app would show exactly one palette per day.

M18 mockups, round 4 — Forecast is a new concept, not a re-skin: the day presented as a weather report, because VOICE.md's whole stance is "readings are weather, not verdicts." Current-conditions hero (composite glyph: the day's animal + its element badge — 60 pairings, all from existing assets), a dashed amber advisory for the clash, the week as a forecast strip (animal + element + tone bar per day), fact tiles the way weather apps do UV/wind, then the full report as prose. Skinned in the Dribbble DNA: cream ground, black anchors, icon tiles, dotted accents.

M18 mockups, round 3 — Compass II translates the owner's Dribbble picks (DailyMe, AI Food Scanner, Rovio, MediCover, education dashboard, mood tracker) into Daymaster: warm cream ground, the element compass as a petal diagram (the food-scanner flower, reinterpreted) with today's dragon at its center, one black ink anchor object per screen (selected week day, agency card, floating nav), a serif-italic run inside the headline (DailyMe's device — we already have deterministic headlineRuns), and dotted outlines for neutral/dashed accents. Rounds 1–2 kept for comparison below.
