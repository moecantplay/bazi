Effects exploration — one layout, five surface treatments. This file isolates a single variable: material/surface effects. All five tabs share one layout skeleton (Daybreak's, from today-three-treatments.html) and the exact Fixture A copy (headline, pillar, favors/watch, roots/day/year reading, agency line, 47-day streak) — nothing here re-litigates layout. Findings are from docs/research-2026-07-30-effects-glass-neumorphism.md and docs/research-2026-07-30-effects-gradient-other.md; read those first for the full argument.

Baseline is the control: Daybreak's layout exactly as it exists in the source file, zero new effects, plus two additions needed so the other four tabs have something to act on — a bottom nav (Daybreak alone among the six treatments had none, and tab 2 needed real chrome to demo restrained glass on) and an "orbit badge" wrapping the existing datebar logo in a circle (so "the hero orbit-ring badge" the research docs discuss has a concrete target). Both additions use the app's own already-verified tokens (var(--ink)/var(--bg) for the nav's anchor contrast, which already flips paper/ink correctly between themes per the 2026-07-30 rule) rather than introducing new ones.

Glass accents applies backdrop-filter glass to exactly two places, per the research doc's restrained verdict: the bottom nav and the week-strip's icon chips. Both sit over a background the page itself controls (not a photo, not another glass layer), and no body text touches the glass — nav labels sit on a solid color-mix(var(--ink)) base strong enough that its worst-case blend against the app's own background range still clears AA (verified below), and week-cell icons are graphical, not text. A `prefers-reduced-transparency`/`prefers-contrast` fallback swaps both to solid `var(--card)`. Hero and reading prose are untouched, per the doc's explicit "never on reading sections" rule.

Gradient extended propagates the app's existing data-driven hero gradient (`.hero-card` in apps/web's globals.css — radial pools keyed to the day's element, already shipped) to the week strip: each cell's icon chip gets its own two-point mesh wash keyed to that day's branch element (reusing the same water/wood/wood/fire/fire/earth/earth assignment the Compass treatment already established in the source file for this fixture week, so today's Wood reading and today's cell agree). The orbit badge also gets the one-time settle-in morph the gradient research doc recommends, playing whenever this tab is shown (gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`).

Neumorphic accent touches only the orbit badge, using the "2.0" hybrid the neumorphism research calls the only defensible use: a soft dual-shadow riding on top of a real `var(--hair)` outline, so the affordance is still carried by an actual border rather than by the shadow alone. It never reaches the favors/watch board or the nav — those keep their normal solid surfaces, exactly per the doc's warning that ambiguous affordance is worst on dense, multi-control screens like this one.

Overdone (why not) is the counter-example the brief asked for, flagged in-UI (not just here): frosted glass stacked on every surface including the hero itself (directly under the headline, with no scrim, opacity deliberately left low), the favors/watch board given neumorphic dual shadows with its outline removed so tappable columns become indistinguishable from decoration, and the nav/week glass pushed to a heavier blur with a weaker base tint. None of this is corrected. It exists to make the failure modes legible on-device rather than only on paper.

Contrast verified (computed, not eyeballed), both themes: ink/bg 14.9:1 (L) · 15.0:1 (D); mut/bg 4.65:1 (L) · 5.88:1 (D); wood-text/bg 4.66:1 (L) · 7.65:1 (D); earth-text/bg 4.65:1 (L) · 8.03:1 (D) — all inherited unchanged from the source file's own round-5c audit. New pairs introduced here: nav label (var(--bg)) against the glass tab's 78%-opacity color-mix(var(--ink)) base, tested against the brightest realistic backdrop (the page's own light --bg, and the hero's wood wash) — 7.4–7.9:1 light, 9.4:1 dark; week-cell icon (var(--ink)) against the glass tab's 14%-opacity chip fill — 11.3:1 light, 10.4:1 dark. These bound the worst case for the solid parts of the glass recipe; the blurred region itself is, by the research doc's own finding, not something a static check can fully guarantee frame-to-frame — which is exactly why no body text sits directly on it anywhere except the deliberately-uncorrected Overdone tab.

Fonts: inlines the same Figtree variable woff2 the source file uses, so this stays self-contained with no external requests.

Recommendation: carry Gradient extended into a real mockup round — it is pure extension of a mechanism already shipped and reviewed, costs nothing in contrast risk, and the research doc rates it the highest-value item in the whole space. Neumorphic accent is a reasonable one-line addition to whichever hero treatment wins, but it's a detail, not a decision worth its own round. Glass accents is defensible but not a strong recommendation on its own merits — it doesn't fail, but the two places it's allowed to touch here are minor enough that shipping it is a coin flip the owner should make deliberately, not a default.
